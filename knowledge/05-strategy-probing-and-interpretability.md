# Strategy Probing and Interpretability for a UTTT-Zero Agent

This note surveys how researchers extract human-understandable knowledge from
AlphaZero-style agents, cheaper non-neural analysis methods, what is already known about
Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe (UTTT) strategy, and a concrete analysis plan. "UTTT" here means this
project's ruleset: closed boards (won/full) can't be played in; being sent to a closed
board grants a free move anywhere; three boards in a row wins; otherwise most-won-boards
wins (a real tiebreak, not a soft draw).

## 1. Interpretability literature on AlphaZero-style agents

- **Linear concept probing on the residual stream** — [McGrath et al. 2022, "Acquisition of chess knowledge in AlphaZero" (PNAS)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2206625119) ([arXiv:2111.09259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09259)). Trains linear probes on activations at every layer/checkpoint to predict ~30 human concepts (material, mobility, king safety), producing "what–when–where" plots. Findings: concepts emerge in a reproducible order (material → mobility → abstract), with a sharp "rapid discovery" window where opening preferences jump too; AlphaZero's first-move policy *narrows* with training (entropy 4.32→2.86 bits) while human opening theory *broadened* over centuries (0.33→1.87 bits); linear regression of concepts onto the value head recovers an interpretable decomposition of what it weighs. **Needs**: full activations at every checkpoint, a programmatic concept-label generator. **UTTT fit**: high — concepts (open lines, board ownership, free-move availability) are computable directly from board state, no external oracle (Stockfish) required. Risk: a small net may saturate concepts almost immediately, compressing the informative emergence timeline.

- **Unsupervised concept discovery + human transfer** — [Schut et al. 2023/2025, "Bridging the human–AI knowledge gap..." (PNAS)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2406675122) ([arXiv:2310.16410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16410)). Uses convex optimization over activations to discover concept *directions* without predefined labels, filtered by teachability and novelty, then validated as puzzles on human experts — four chess grandmasters improved after training on AI-discovered concepts. **Needs**: activations, a candidate-position pool, human validators (the expensive, non-automatable step). **UTTT fit**: discovery half transfers cleanly; validation is easier (no grandmasters needed) but "novelty" needs a different baseline than "human theory," since UTTT has no comparable expert corpus — a strong heuristic or minimax-derived prior could substitute.

- **Reliability critique of probing** — [Pálsson et al. 2023, IJCAI](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0541.pdf) reproduces McGrath-style probing on open Leela-derived nets; the follow-up [Pálsson et al. 2024, "Empirical Evaluation of Concept Probing for Game-Playing Agents"](https://ebooks.iospress.nl/doi/10.3233/FAIA240574) shows **linear-probe accuracy is an unreliable proxy for causal importance** and recommends more complex probes plus amnesic (nullspace-projection) probing. Key caveat for any UTTT probing work.

- **Learned look-ahead (Leela Chess Zero)** — [Jenner et al. 2024, NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00877) ([project page](https://leela-interp.github.io/)). Using activation patching, they find the target square of the move *two plies ahead* carries outsized causal weight; identify an attention head that moves this information "backward in time" (zeroing one entry collapses move probability from ~50% to ~18% in >10% of puzzles); train a bilinear probe predicting the 2-ply-ahead move at 92% accuracy (vs. 15% on a randomly initialized control). **Needs**: a per-square/token architectural correspondence, positions with a single clear best line, patched forward passes. **UTTT fit**: the specific per-square-token trick doesn't map cleanly onto a convolutional net, but the general recipe (patch → rank causal effect → probe motivated by the patching result → randomly-initialized control) is architecture-agnostic and portable.

- **Emergent world models (Othello-GPT)** — [Li et al., "Emergent world representations..."](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382). A GPT trained only to predict legal Othello moves develops an internal board representation — but only a **non-linear** probe finds it (linear probes fail), in a "mine/theirs" relative frame. Interventions on the probe direction causally change predicted legal moves ("latent saliency maps"). Warns against assuming linear-decodability by default; UTTT probing should try both linear and shallow non-linear probes.

- **Hex: emergence order and probe+behavior pairing** — [Lovering et al. 2022, NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14673) / [Forde et al., AAAI-22 workshop](https://cs.brown.edu/people/gdk/pubs/concepts_hex_aaaiws.pdf). Close in board scale to UTTT. Findings: **MCTS discovers concepts before the raw network encodes them** (probing the net alone can understate what the full agent knows); short-term/endgame concepts localize to late layers, long-term/opening concepts to middle layers. Template: pair **behavioral tests** (does manipulating a concept change the move choice) with **probing** (is it linearly decodable) — the two can diverge.

- **Natural-language annotations as concept source** — [Tomlin, He, Klein 2022, ACL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.07531). Uses 10K human-annotated Go comments as free concept labels, probing for mentions of terms like "ko"/"atari"; concepts are more predictable from later layers. No equivalent UTTT commentary corpus exists, but self-generated LLM narration of self-play games could substitute.

- **Open-source reproduction + information-flow saliency** — [Hammersborg et al. 2023, Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-45225-x). Reproduces concept detection on open Leela nets and introduces a saliency method with information-flow guarantees (exhaustively/exclusively identifies used input information) — directly reusable and a good fit for UTTT's small discrete input encoding.

- **General game concept taxonomy** — [Piette, Stephenson, Soemers, Browne 2021, IEEE CoG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01078), built on [Ludii](https://ludii.games/) (which already implements UTTT). A ready-made, game-agnostic concept vocabulary/computation engine — could save engineering effort versus hand-designing UTTT concept detectors.

- **MCTS-side explainability (not network-side)** — [Baier & Kaisers 2020, AAAI-21](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/30850/30850.pdf); [Sironi & Baier, "Toward Template-Free Explainability for MCTS"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16524). Computes cheap search-tree statistics (visit-weighted alternative-line summaries, value spread) to explain a single decision, nearly free since MCTS already computes them. Complements, rather than replaces, concept probing.

## 2. Non-neural, cheaper analysis methods (agent as oracle)

These treat the agent as a black box and analyze behavior, not internals — cheaper, more robust, but answer "what" rather than "how."

**(a) Opening-tree / opening-book extraction.** [Sadler & Regan, *Game Changer* (New in Chess, 2019)](https://www.newinchess.com/game-changer) used AlphaZero's raw search trees (node counts, priors, post-search evals) to write human commentary on entire opening systems (a dedicated section covers AlphaZero's opening repertoire, e.g. the King's Indian Sämisch and the Carlsbad). The [AlphaZero paper (Silver et al. 2018)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aar6404) reports, per popular human opening, self-play adoption curves, W/D/L vs. Stockfish, and principal variations — a machine-generated opening book with evaluation attached. [KataGo's opening books](https://katagobooks.org/) do the same for small-board Go. For UTTT itself, [minimax.dev's solver project](https://minimax.dev/docs/ultimate/) uses proof-number/depth-first proof-number search (PN/DFPN), reaching exhaustive solved status to ~20 ply, and its [pruning analysis](https://minimax.dev/docs/ultimate/pruning/) documents concrete UTTT facts (never send the opponent to a board where one move wins the whole game) independent of any neural net. Cheap if search infrastructure already exists.

**(b) Statistical mining of self-play corpora.** [Addison, Peeler, Alvin, "Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe Bot Techniques" (FLAIRS-35, 2022)](https://journals.flvc.org/FLAIRS/article/view/130698) pits random/heatmap/MCTS/AlphaZero-style bots against each other over tens of thousands of games, finding a positional **heatmap** (empirical cell-occupation value across games) more informative than shallow MCTS statistics given UTTT's lack of an obvious material heuristic. The [gPress opening survey](https://gpress.soopergrape.com/index.php/2025/06/26/n-in-a-row-games-part-3/) tabulates engine (uttt.ai) evaluation scores per named opening (Center-Center +11.81, Center-Corner +11.07, etc.) — exactly the kind of first-move value table this project should reproduce. Very cheap: pure post-hoc analysis of logged games (win rate by opening, 3-in-a-row vs. tiebreak wins, mean closed boards per game, free-move frequency, order boards are won, center-ownership/outcome correlation) — no network internals needed. Highest value-per-effort in this whole survey.

**(c) Counterfactual/ablation on the value function.** Perturb one factor at a time in real or synthetic positions (flip center-macro-cell ownership, force-close a board, add/remove a free-move opportunity) and measure the shift in the value head's evaluation — McGrath's and Jenner's general recipe, pared to something requiring only forward-pass access (no activations, no probes). Directly answers "how many win-probability points is center ownership worth" and "what is tempo worth." Chess analogue: [Serra Peralta & Vilalta, "The Value of Chess Squares" (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05330), which fits per-square value heatmaps to grandmaster games via essentially the same counterfactual logic.

**(d) Distilling into interpretable surrogates.** [Bastani, Pu, Solar-Lezama, VIPER (NeurIPS 2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08328) — a DAGGER-style imitation loop trains a compact decision tree to mimic a deep RL policy/value function, enabling verification while measuring the strength gap. Extensions: mixture-of-expert trees ([MoËT](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BJlxdCVKDB)), multi-agent ([MAVIPER](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-26412-2_16)). [Landajuela et al. 2021](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/landajuela21a/landajuela21a.pdf) fits closed-form symbolic-regression formulas instead of trees. For UTTT: distill against hand-crafted features (macro-cell status, line counts, free-move indicators) and measure playing strength retained versus the full network.

**(e) "What does the net see": entropy, phase, surprise.** McGrath's first-move entropy trajectory is one instance of a broader cheap technique: bucket policy entropy by game phase or move number. [A 2025 follow-up, "The Algorithm Is Not the Behavior"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21380) examines layer-wise entropy and divergence from the final policy, finding entropy dips mid-game and rises in the endgame. Cheapest of all: log **root-value vs. raw-prior disagreement** during self-play — positions where the network's intuition and full-search evaluation diverge sharply are free-to-mine "surprise" positions (paralleling *Game Changer*'s examples of AlphaZero's top-prior move not being its top-evaluated move).

Scaffolding references: [Victor Allis's 1988 Connect Four solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connect_Four) (nine named winning-strategy patterns) shows what a *complete* symbolic strategic account of a UTTT-scale game could look like if fully solved; MCTS-explainability work (section 1) turns search statistics into natural-language decision explanations.

## 3. What is already known about UTTT strategy

**Rules caveat first.** [Bertholon et al., "At Most 43 Moves, At Least 29..." (2020, arXiv:2006.02353)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02353) proves a **forced first-player win** — but under a *different* ruleset, where a player sent to a decided board must still play any remaining empty cell there, rather than moving freely. Under that rule they give an explicit winning strategy (center-of-center opening, then always answer in the center of whichever board you're sent to) winning in ≤43 moves, a second-player strategy surviving ≥29 moves, and a proof that the optimal first two moves form a specific "double." [Diamond 2022 (arXiv:2207.06239)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06239) proposes randomizing the first four moves as a fix (odds of recreating the exploit: ~0.09%). Independently, [Joachim Breitner's 2013 post](https://www.joachim-breitner.de/blog/604-Ultimate_Tic_Tac_Toe_is_always_won_by_X) and [Ben Orlin's original "Math with Bad Drawings" post](https://mathwithbaddrawings.com/ultimate-tic-tac-toe-original-post/) found essentially the same forced win years earlier under the same rule — and Orlin's own errata give the fix as exactly this project's rule: *"if you are sent to a board that's already been won, you may go wherever you like."* **This project's ruleset is the "fixed" variant; its exact game-theoretic value is not proven anywhere found**, and the [minimax.dev](https://minimax.dev/docs/ultimate/) proof-number solver (built for the free-move rules) estimates a full solve at ~$2M–$10M of compute, i.e., out of reach — treat first-player-advantage claims for this ruleset as empirical pending this project's own data.

**Opening choices.** [BoardGameGeek/StackExchange](https://boardgames.stackexchange.com/questions/49291/strategy-for-ultimate-tic-tac-toe), [Rare Pike](https://rarepike.com/three/ultimate-tic-tac-toe/), [TicToe.org](https://tictoe.org/games/ultimate-tic-tac-toe), and [gPress](https://gpress.soopergrape.com/index.php/2025/06/26/n-in-a-row-games-part-3/) converge on the **center cell of the center board** ("Center-Center"/"Main Line") as strongest or joint-strongest, closely followed by "Center-Corner"; engine scores cited by gPress rate them within a few tenths of a point (+11.81 vs. +11.07).

**Tempo / free-move value.** [gPress](https://gpress.soopergrape.com/index.php/2025/06/26/n-in-a-row-games-part-3/) names "home square" (keeps play on the same board) and "free move square" (grants the opponent a free choice); [minimax.dev's pruning page](https://minimax.dev/docs/ultimate/pruning/) formalizes the hard constraint that a player must never send the opponent to a board where a single move wins the game outright. [BoardGameGeek](https://boardgames.stackexchange.com/questions/49291/strategy-for-ultimate-tic-tac-toe) reports actual bot heuristic weights: small-board win 5 pts, center-board win 10, corner-board win 3, center square of any small board 3, being granted a free move 2 — a concrete if informal quantification of tempo's value relative to material.

**Sacrifice / forcing concepts.** Ben Orlin's ["Orlin Gambit"](https://mathwithbaddrawings.com/ultimate-tic-tac-toe-original-post/) — repeatedly playing the center of whatever board you're sent to, deliberately sacrificing the center macro-board for strong uncontested position in the other eight — is the most widely cited named UTTT idea, echoing the general principle (also in [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_tic-tac-toe) and [Rare Pike](https://rarepike.com/three/ultimate-tic-tac-toe/)) that a move's purpose in UTTT is rarely to win the local board it's played in.

**Center and corner macro-board value.** Sources uniformly rate the center macro-board highest (feeds four macro-lines) and advise against being the one to grant the opponent access to it; corner macro-boards outrank edge ones, both by analogy to plain tic-tac-toe and because corners feed diagonal macro-lines that [Rare Pike](https://rarepike.com/three/ultimate-tic-tac-toe/) says opponents often underweight.

**Tiebreak-by-most-boards.** This project's no-draw, most-boards-wins rule is less commonly documented; most casual descriptions ([Orlin](https://mathwithbaddrawings.com/ultimate-tic-tac-toe-original-post/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_tic-tac-toe)) treat a fully-played, unwon board as an outright draw. No source quantifies how often a most-boards tiebreak would actually decide a game, or whether it changes optimal sacrifice decisions — an open question for this project's own corpus.

**Difficulty and first-player edge in practice.** Both [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_tic-tac-toe) and the [FLAIRS paper](https://journals.flvc.org/FLAIRS/article/view/130698) note UTTT "lacks any simple heuristic evaluation function" — naive heuristics underperform badly, shallow MCTS barely beats random, while a trained AlphaZero-style CNN dominates (88% win rate vs. the field) — evidence that a learned agent, not hand-engineering or brute-force solving, is the practical route to strategic insight at this game's scale.

## Proposed analysis program

Ordered cheapest/most-robust to most ambitious.

1. **Self-play corpus statistics (cheap, robust — do first).** Data: logged finished games only, no network internals. Answers: win rate by first move, 3-in-a-row vs. tiebreak win frequency, mean closed boards per game, free-move frequency/outcome, order boards are won, center/corner-ownership correlation with outcome.
2. **Opening value heatmap + shallow PV tree.** Data: trained net + fixed MCTS budget over all 81 first moves to depth ~4–6. Answers: agent's first-move rankings vs. the human opening table in §3; where prior and full-search top move diverge.
3. **Root-value vs. raw-prior disagreement mining.** Data: log network prior alongside post-search value/move during self-play (no extra compute). Answers: which positions the agent's "intuition" gets most wrong relative to search; entropy by game phase.
4. **Counterfactual/ablation sweeps on the value head.** Data: forward-pass access + a position perturbation generator. Answers: quantified win-probability value of center ownership, corner vs. edge ownership, free-move grants, and forced-board tempo.
5. **Hand-crafted-feature distillation.** Data: trained net as labeling oracle + a small feature set (macro-cell status, line counts, free-move/tempo indicators from #4). Answers: how much strength a legible tree/linear model retains, and which features it weights most.
6. **Linear concept probing on residual-stream activations (ambitious).** Data: activation dumps across layers/checkpoints + programmatically computed concept labels (no external oracle needed, unlike chess/Stockfish). Answers: McGrath-style what–when–where map for UTTT concepts. Pair with #4's ablations given the Pálsson et al. (2024) caveat that probe accuracy ≠ causal importance.
7. **Causal/mechanistic look-ahead study (ambitious).** Data: activations + curated forced-line positions + patched forward passes. Answers: whether the net internally represents future forced-sequence consequences, adapted from Jenner et al.'s transformer-specific method to a convolutional architecture.
8. **Unsupervised concept discovery (most ambitious).** Data: everything in #6 + a convex-search procedure + human UTTT players for puzzle validation (no grandmaster-equivalent pool exists, so amateur/strong-player validation substitutes). Answers: whether the agent has found UTTT ideas absent from the informal human strategy corpus in §3, using a strong-heuristic or solver baseline in place of "human theory" for the novelty criterion.

## Key takeaways

- UTTT's strategy literature is thin and informal (blogs, StackExchange, one FLAIRS bot paper); this project's self-play corpus will likely produce the first systematic answers to several open questions.
- Bertholon et al.'s (2020) forced-win proof applies to a **stricter ruleset** (must-stay-in-decided-board) than this project's (free-move-anywhere) — do not cite it as proving first-player win here; the free-move variant is unsolved and effectively unsolvable by brute force at present cost.
- Cheap behavioral methods (self-play statistics, opening heatmaps, root-vs-search disagreement logs) should come first: no network internals needed, most robust to reruns, and answer most standard UTTT questions directly.
- Counterfactual ablation on the value head is the best tool for turning folk wisdom ("center control matters," "tempo matters") into quantified win-probability deltas, with no interpretability infrastructure beyond forward passes.
- Linear concept probing is comparatively cheap on a small UTTT net since concept labels are programmatically computable from board state — a good "ambitious but tractable" second tier.
- Biggest caveat (Pálsson et al. 2024; Othello-GPT's linear-probe failure): **probe accuracy is not causal importance** — a concept can be decodable without being used, or used without being linearly decodable. Always pair probing with an ablation or behavioral test before claiming the network "relies on" a concept.
- The Hex studies' finding that MCTS can encode a concept before the raw network does means bare-network probing can understate what the full agent (net + search) knows — evaluate against full-search behavior when search is available.
- Unsupervised concept discovery (Schut et al.) has the highest ceiling (can surface genuinely new ideas, not just confirm known ones) but both its "novelty" and "human expert" baselines need substitutes for UTTT, given the thin existing strategy corpus.
- Mechanistic look-ahead studies (Jenner et al.) are the hardest to port: their core trick relies on a transformer's clean square-to-token correspondence, which a convolutional UTTT net lacks without adaptation.
- Policy distillation into trees/formulas doubles as an interpretability method and a strength benchmark — the strength gap to the full network quantifies how much of its play is captured by the hand-named concepts already known from human UTTT discussion.
